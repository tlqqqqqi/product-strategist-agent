# Case types

Every case is a decision about an existing product. Classify it at intake; the type sets the
default criteria and the questions most likely to flip the choice.

## Diagnosis ("a metric moved, what do we do")

- First separate the symptom from the cause. A same-month coincidence (a release, a price
  change, a campaign) is a hypothesis until a cut of the data isolates it.
- Cut the metric by cohort, channel, segment and plan before blaming any single change. A drop
  concentrated in one cohort points away from changes that hit everyone.
- Typical flip questions: where is the change concentrated; did it start before or after the
  suspected event; is the metric definition or tracking unchanged; what do leavers say.
- Default criteria: effect on the metric, cost and time, reversibility, risk to other metrics.

## Market entry ("should we launch X / enter segment Y")

- Size the reachable segment from sources, not from top-down market totals.
- Compare against the alternatives the audience already uses, including doing nothing.
- Typical flip questions: is there demand at our price; who already serves it and how; what
  does it take to be chosen; regulation.
- Default criteria: reachable demand, fit with the current product, cost to enter, time to
  first revenue, strategic fit.

## Messaging ("how do we talk about X")

- The product decides what can be promised; the message only chooses what to say first.
- Typical flip questions: what barrier stops the audience; which proof the audience trusts;
  which promises competitors already own.
- Default criteria: relevance to the barrier, provability, distinctness from competitors,
  cost of the channels.

## Options every case gets

"Keep as is" and "close / stop investing" are always on the list. They anchor the others: an
option that cannot beat "keep as is" on the criteria is not worth its cost.
