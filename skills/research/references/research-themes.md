# Research themes

A decision is only as wide as what was looked at. The user's materials say what happened inside
the product; the web says what is normal, what others did, and what changed outside. Pick the
themes that could change the choice in this case: at least 3 themes and at least 4 web
questions (the Gate 1 check counts them), and more when the case is open or the stakes are high.
Write each in plan.md as `тема: <theme>; где искать: веб, <what pages>`.

| Theme (`тема:`) | What it answers | Where it usually is |
|---|---|---|
| рынок | Size, growth, shifts in demand and prices in the category this year | Industry reviews, analyst notes, statistics agencies, company reports |
| конкуренты | What direct rivals offer and charge, what they changed lately | Rivals' tariff and help pages, release notes, press releases |
| аналоги | How products in neighbouring categories or other countries solved the same problem | Case studies, product blogs, conference talks |
| бенчмарки | What "normal" looks like: churn, conversion, retention, onboarding completion | Industry benchmark reports, investor letters, research by analytics vendors |
| голос пользователей | Why people join, stay and leave, in their own words | App store reviews, forums, social posts, review sites |
| внешние причины | Seasonality, macro, regulation, platform rules, ad market changes | News, regulators, platform policy pages, ad network announcements |
| каналы и привлечение | Quality of traffic by channel, what paid acquisition does to retention | Marketing benchmarks, ad network studies, growth blogs |
| исследования | Evidence on the mechanism itself: trials, onboarding, pricing psychology | Papers, academic and industry studies (English is fine) |
| технологии и регулирование | Constraints that limit an option: law, standards, platform fees | Laws, regulators, platform documentation |

## How to work a theme (several rounds, not one search)

1. **Decompose.** Turn the question into 3-5 sub-questions: a number, a comparison, a cause, a
   counter-example.
2. **Round 1, broad.** One search per sub-question, mixing kinds: `semantic` for the plain-words
   question, `keyword` for names and terms, `text` for numbers. Save every page you will quote.
3. **Round 2, follow the leads.** Read what you saved; search again with the names, terms and
   numbers you found there (a report title, a competitor's tariff name, an author). Look for a
   source that disagrees.
4. **Stop** when a new search brings nothing new for two queries in a row, or the answer can no
   longer change the ranking. What stays unknown becomes a `gap` claim.

The Gate 2 check counts per web question at least 4 searches over at least 2 kinds and at least
4 saved pages, and for the case at least 8 facts quoted from web pages of at least 5 different
sites. These are floors: a case where the answer is contested deserves more.
